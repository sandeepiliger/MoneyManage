# What to build next

Ordered by what would actually change a user's experience, not by what is easiest.

## Before anything else

Finish v1. [KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md](KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md) has the list — compile it, lint it, run it,
and either implement the FAMILY tier or take it off the paywall. Nothing below matters until the
app runs.

---

## 1. Home-screen widgets and a quick-add tile

The core loop is "record an expense in two seconds". Opening the app is most of those two seconds.

- A widget showing today's spend and a `+` that goes straight to the keypad.
- A quick-settings tile.
- Support for Android's app shortcuts on long-press.

This is the highest-leverage thing left. Everything else is a feature; this makes the feature people
already have measurably faster.

## 2. Notification-based import

SMS is being deprecated as a bank notification channel — several Indian banks already push app
notifications and nothing else. A `NotificationListenerService` would cover them.

Gate it exactly as SMS is gated: off by default, opt-in, parsed on-device, landing as pending. The
permission is more alarming to users than SMS, so the explanation screen matters more.

## 3. Receipt attachments

The table and the entitlement exist. What is needed: a camera and gallery picker, storage in the
app's private files (never in shared media, where a gallery app would show someone's receipts to
anyone scrolling), a thumbnail on the transaction row, and inclusion in the backup as separate files
alongside the JSON.

Consider on-device OCR (ML Kit) to prefill the amount. Behind the `FinancialAiService` abstraction
or its own, so it stays swappable — and on-device, so a receipt photo never leaves the phone.

## 4. Split transactions

One bill, several categories. A ₹4,000 supermarket trip is groceries *and* household *and* a gift.
Users work around this by entering three transactions, which breaks the reconciliation against a
single bank line.

The model change is real: a transaction gains child splits, and `BalanceCalculator`,
`CashflowAnalyzer` and `BudgetCalculator` all need to roll them up correctly. `Money.allocate()`
already exists and distributes a remainder without losing paise. Do this one with tests first.

## 5. Real dashboard reordering

Order is stored and read; the drag-to-reorder UI was never built. `DashboardCard.DEFAULT_ORDER`
and the hidden-card set are already plumbed through.

## 6. Custom date ranges

The Pro entitlement `CUSTOM_DATE_RANGES` is sold and unimplemented. `DateRange` already handles
arbitrary ranges — this is a picker and a chip, not a subsystem.

## Worth considering, with reservations

### Multi-currency

`Money` is currency-typed and mixed arithmetic already throws, so the foundation is sound. What is
missing is exchange rates — which means a network dependency, a rate source, and a decision about
whether to store the rate at transaction time (correct, and what accounting requires) or convert on
display (simpler, and wrong the moment a rate moves).

Store the rate on the transaction. The alternative makes last year's reports change when the rupee
does.

Real demand for this in an India-first app is probably NRIs and frequent travellers. Worth
measuring before building.

### Database encryption

Reconsider if any of these become true: the app starts storing something more sensitive than a
ledger; a compliance requirement appears; or SQLCipher's cost on low-end devices drops. The
reasoning against it today is in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) and is about cost, not principle.

### Cloud AI

The abstraction is ready and the local engine covers the questions people actually ask. Build the
backend when there is evidence users are asking things rules cannot answer — and put the key on
your server, never in the APK.

## What to leave alone

The temptation with a v2 is to add the things competitors have. These are the ones to resist,
because they would each undo something this app is.

**Cloud sync and accounts.** The absence of a server *is* the product. Adding sync means adding
accounts, a breach surface, a privacy policy that says "we hold your financial data", and a Data
Safety form that stops saying "collects nothing". If users want their data on two devices, make
backup and restore excellent — that is a smaller problem and it keeps the promise.

**Bank account aggregation.** Account Aggregator / UPI-linked balance fetching would remove manual
entry entirely, and it would mean holding consent artefacts, a server, and a regulatory posture.
For a solo or small team this changes what the product *is*. If you do it, do it deliberately, and
rewrite [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md) honestly.

**Gamification.** Streaks, badges, points. They lift retention metrics and they change what the app
feels like: a tool for seeing your money becomes an app that wants you to open it. The tone
throughout — "over budget" as a fact, never a scold; a month with a wedding in it is not a failure —
is deliberate and would not survive it.

**More ads, or ads in more places.** The placement list in `AdPlacement` is short on purpose, and
`ADMOB.md` records why each excluded spot is excluded. That list erodes one screen at a time if
nobody defends it.

**Deeper category nesting.** Users ask for it. It reads as flexibility until you are three taps into
a picker at a shop counter, and it makes every report harder to read. Two levels is the constraint
that keeps categorising a one-tap decision.

**Auto-posting recurring transactions by default.** The one-line reason: the app knows rent was
*due*, not that it went out. A balance the user cannot reconcile against their bank statement is
worse than no balance.
